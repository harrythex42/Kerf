CEDARWAY MOBILITY / SERVICE PLANNING

Service readiness review

North district shuttle timetable · August 2026 · Readiness decision brief

Decision and scope

Cedarway plans to introduce a revised weekday timetable for the North district shuttle. The new schedule retains the first and last trips and adds a timed transfer at Market Junction. It does not change the published weekend service. The proposed start date is the first Monday after schools reopen.

The readiness group recommends proceeding if the transfer test passes and the two accessible reserve vehicles remain available through the first fortnight. If either condition fails, move the change to the following Monday and keep the current timetable in customer information channels.

What changed after consultation

Riders asked the team to preserve the earlier arrival at the health centre and reduce long waits at the junction. The original proposal removed an early stop because that trip averaged fewer than four boardings. The revised proposal preserves the stop and adjusts the road segment instead.

The consultation team also received requests for a later Sunday journey. That request falls outside the current change and will be considered with the winter timetable. Customer information staff will keep the scope explicit when responding to these requests.

Operating plan

The revised plan uses the same number of scheduled vehicles. The main change is where drivers take their recovery time. The duty schedule moves one recovery interval from River Gate to Market Junction so that a late feeder can still make the timed transfer when traffic permits.

Drivers and dispatch

Dispatch will monitor departures during the first hour of weekday service. Drivers should report a missed transfer using the normal incident code rather than create a separate alert. The duty coordinator will record which route was late and whether a connection could safely be held.

The regular dispatch rota covers the first week. A route supervisor will join the morning briefing for the first three days and will remain on call if a stop sign, driver card or real-time display disagrees with the approved timetable.

Equipment and accessibility

Each vehicle on the revised rota must complete the normal ramp and audible announcement checks. The reserve vehicles must carry current destination settings and remain available to both depots. A booking team member will ride the first service and check that the published transfer instructions match the signage.

Customer information

The communications team maintains one approved schedule from which it produces stop notices, the web timetable and the operator briefing. Old posters will be collected during the changeover. Where a poster cannot be replaced before service starts, the local supervisor will add a dated temporary notice.

Support staff will have the updated route card before the first public announcement. The card explains which journeys changed and where customers should wait. It also lists the contact route for passenger access issues, separate from the contact for timetable corrections.

Changeover decision

The controller signs off in stages. A failed rehearsal returns the timetable to its owner for correction; it does not advance to public release. The diagram combines the approval, publication and first-day verification steps so that a handover can identify where the decision is held.

Recording the decision

Store the signed decision with the rehearsal log and the final public timetable. If the release is deferred, record the outstanding item, its owner and the next decision time. Customer information should not change until a controller confirms the start date.
