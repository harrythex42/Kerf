HARBORLIGHT SERVICES / CUSTOMER OPERATIONS

Support transition review

June 2026 · Six-week review of the transition to a shared support desk

Summary

Harborlight moved routine support requests from regional email queues to one shared desk. The desk now receives account, scheduling and service requests for all four districts. Daytime coverage stayed the same; the change was intended to make ownership visible and reduce handovers, not to extend hours.

The desk accepted 1,746 requests in its first six weeks. Median assignment time fell from 47 minutes to 21 minutes. Resolution time was broadly unchanged. Most of the improvement came from assigning general enquiries earlier; specialist repair requests still depended on district availability.

Decision requested

Keep the shared desk as the default intake route and retain a named liaison in each district for another month. The liaison should review recurring transfer reasons with the desk lead each Friday, rather than reintroduce a parallel queue.

Intake and assignment

A desk coordinator checks that each request has a service reference and a named contact. The coordinator can solve documented account questions directly. Requests that require a field visit move to a district team with a short reason for transfer.

The request stays in the shared desk until the receiving team accepts it. This matters when a ticket mentions several locations or a vendor with more than one service contract. The originating coordinator remains responsible for the customer update until an owner is recorded.

Transfer quality

In week one, the district teams rejected 38 transfers because the location or contract reference was missing. By week six, they rejected nine. The biggest improvements were a visible location field and a short note explaining why a request could not be completed at the desk.

Some tickets were forwarded correctly but reopened after the customer replied to an old regional address. Operations will keep those addresses active until the remaining service notices have been updated. The desk lead reviews messages forwarded from them twice a day.

Coverage and continuity

The shared rota places two coordinators on early and late cover. District specialists are still scheduled by their local teams. This arrangement worked during ordinary absences, but one training afternoon left only a single coordinator available for the last two hours.

Escalation

Urgent requests are assigned directly to the duty specialist. If the specialist cannot accept, the desk calls the district liaison rather than transferring the request to an unmonitored queue. The escalation list is reviewed at the start of each week.

What customers noticed

Customer Success interviewed eight account contacts. Most reported that they no longer needed to guess which region to email. Two wanted a clearer message after a specialist transfer; both had received a new ticket number without a sentence linking it to the old one.

The next copy revision will show the existing reference and say who owns the request. It will not promise a resolution time when the district has yet to confirm availability. The team will compare reopened requests before and after updating the message.
